# The cabinet — 2026-08-06

Working document. Not published to the site. **Not yet publishable** — see §5.

A gallery of specimens: systems that already exist, measured with the program's own
instruments. Not sources, not imports, not influences. Objects.

The purpose is calibration. `code/README.md` states the discipline for the numerical
instruments — point them at a Sierpiński gasket, whose spectral dimension is known,
and only then at anything unknown. The conceptual instruments have never been
calibrated that way. τ, closure, harvestability, and the assertion/residue line have
only ever been pointed at the program's own constructions. This is where they get
pointed at things whose answers are already known, to see whether they return sensible
verdicts.

A gallery that only admires is the harvest line's named failure mode with the paper
stripped off. Hence §1.

---

## 1. The entry rule

> **An exhibit enters only if the program's instruments return a verdict on it that
> could be wrong.**

Corollaries, stated because each one excludes something currently tempting:

- **A diagnosis, not an appreciation.** "Interesting" is not a verdict. If the label
  could survive the exhibit being entirely different, it is not a label.
- **The instrument must be named.** Which one — τ, closure, borrowed/derived,
  harvestability, ι — and what it returned. An exhibit measured by no instrument is a
  reference, and belongs in a paper's bibliography.
- **Known answers first.** A cabinet composed only of specimens the program diagnoses
  as *failures* is not calibrated, it is a trophy case. It must contain specimens the
  instruments should pass, and it must be possible for one to come back wrong.
- **A wrong verdict is kept, corrected in place.** Same rule as `_archive/` and the
  code: where a measurement was wrong on the first pass, the correction is named rather
  than quietly repaired.

**The disqualifier.** If an exhibit's only function is to show that the program's
vocabulary can be applied to it, remove the exhibit. The vocabulary can be applied to
anything; that is the complaint, not the demonstration.

## 3. Exhibits

### Should pass — calibration specimens

**Proof of work** ✓
σ = dissipated energy · **derived** · τ ≈ 1 for "this work was done", τ ≈ 0 for
anything broader (who did it, why, whether it was useful) · coupled, physically, by
construction.
*Verdict.* The program's origin case and its narrowest one. The trace gap is excellent
for exactly one proposition and worthless for every other, which is why the mechanism
needed nothing else and could say nothing else. **Wrong if** a cheaper forgery of the
dissipation claim exists that isn't simply a 51% attack.

**Termite nest construction (Grassé, 1959)** ✓
σ = accumulated structure · **derived** · τ ≥ 1, the trace *is* the work · coupled to
the physical medium.
*Verdict.* The operator performed sixty years early, in entomology, without the
vocabulary. Already load-bearing in *Sign and Work*. Included as the sematectonic
reference specimen — the case where assertion and residue are the same object.

**Wikipedia's verifiability policy** ~
σ = agreement among editors · coherence, therefore closure-prone · τ low (citations are
cheap to fabricate, expensive to sustain against attention) · **coupled** — the sourcing
requirement is structural coupling to a corpus outside the editors.
*Verdict.* The interesting one, because it is a coherence mechanism that did *not*
close. *ACF* prices escape at d scalars of contact, once; the sourcing rule is a
standing, not a one-time, coupling and it is cheap. If *ACF* §6 is right that
measurement-by-artifact is available coupling, this is the working instance. **Wrong
if** the coupling turns out to be the editor population's turnover rather than the
policy — which would make the policy decorative and the diagnosis backwards.

### Should fail — and do

**Proof of stake** ✓
σ = the coin itself · **borrowed** — the staked quantity is the mechanism's own output ·
τ undefined, there is no trace, only a balance · closed by construction.
*Verdict.* The operator's negative case, and the cleanest one in the cabinet. The
mechanism stakes a quantity it issues. Whatever else is true of it, it is not derived,
and *A Consistent Fiction*'s attractor argument applies without modification.

**The h-index and citation counts** ✓
σ = scholarly contribution · **borrowed** — the proxy is issued by the same community it
scores · τ ≈ 0, citation rings forge it at almost no cost · closed.
*Verdict.* The standard Goodhart specimen. Included because it is the one every reader
already believes, and any instrument that cannot return the right verdict here is
broken.

**LLM benchmark leaderboards** ~
σ = capability · **borrowed** · τ collapsing in real time — training-set contamination
is forgery at negative cost, since the forger is also the party being measured · weakly
coupled, and the coupling (held-out sets) degrades on contact.
*Verdict.* A live τ-collapse with a date on it, which the cabinet otherwise lacks. Every
other specimen here failed historically. **Wrong if** contamination turns out to be
better modelled as an ι failure — capability and benchmark score are not independent
projections — in which case this exhibit belongs to a different instrument and is more
interesting.

**Ptolemaic epicycles** ✓
σ = predictive agreement · coherence · τ ≈ 0, an epicycle can be added to fit anything ·
closed, then coupled out.
*Verdict.* The historical control for *A Consistent Fiction*. A closed system with
*excellent* predictive fit, sustained for centuries, which failed not by predicting
wrongly but by requiring unbounded free parameters. Fit is not coupling. This is the
exhibit that shows closure is compatible with being right about a great deal.

**Timewave Zero** ✓ *(sources checked 2026-08-06; one substantive correction below)*
σ = "novelty" — gestured at negentropy, neither measurable nor provenance-fixed ·
**borrowed**, from the King Wen sequence · τ ≈ 0, every historical fit was retrofitted ·
closed — but see the correction.
*Verdict.* Organizational closure in the wild, unusually well documented: an internally
elaborated criterion, a two-decade run, and a datable coupling event. Watkins met
McKenna in January 1994 and the critique followed; its core is the **half-twist**, a
step reversing the signs of half of the 64 numbers, for which the software manual's own
footnote conceded "the reason for this is not well understood at present and is a
question which awaits further research." Watkins showed the step *prevents* the
geometric property McKenna offered to justify it, and that without it the wave stops
showing the historical correlations claimed for it.

**The correction, and it changes the verdict's target.** The first draft of this exhibit
said the system "absorbed the critique by revision rather than refutation." That is
unfair to McKenna and the sources say so. He could not recall the step's rationale after
twenty years, attempted a justification, had it refuted, and **endorsed publication of
the critique.** His own conduct was the opposite of closed. What routed around the
refutation was the *community* — a defence literature persists, including material
arguing the objection is not fatal. So the specimen is sharper than first written:
**closure is a property of the system, not of the author, and here the author supplied
the coupling and the system rejected it.** That is a better instance of autopoiesis than
a man who would not listen, and *A Consistent Fiction*'s claim that closure is an
attractor rather than a vice is what it illustrates.
*(One thread remains unverified: a revised timewave attributed to John Sheliak is
referenced in secondary sources but not in the primary document checked. Stated as
uncertain rather than dropped.)*
**The redeeming feature, and the reason it is an exhibit rather than a joke:** the
zero-date was falsifiable and went red. By the program's own most-transferable rule —
prefer diagnostics that can return absurdities — Timewave did the one thing a
generative sim structurally struggles to do. A closed system with a terminal falsifiable
prediction is a better specimen than one without.

### Undiagnosed — the instrument returns nothing yet

**Fiat currency.** σ = confidence. Borrowed or derived is genuinely unclear, and that
unclarity is informative rather than a gap in my reading. The instruments do not
currently resolve it and I would rather record that than manufacture a verdict.

**NIST's PQ standardisation.** Already diagnosed inside *Borrowed Again* — the standards
body ran the program's sorting and got hedging rather than migration. Listed for
cross-reference; it is a paper's content, not an exhibit, unless the cabinet is meant to
duplicate.

**Academic peer review.** Obvious candidate, no confident τ. The forging cost of a
referee report is low; the earning cost is also low; the ratio may be near 1 for a
reason that has nothing to do with the mechanism working.

## 4. What the cabinet is for, mechanically

The columns are the program's measured quantities, so the cabinet is a table, and
**empty cells are research**. Three are already visible:

1. No exhibit in the cabinet has a **measured** τ. Every value above is an ordinal
   judgement. *Sign and Work* §7.1 asks for τ on the spectral projection; the cabinet
   asks the prior question — has τ ever been measured on anything at all? One number
   exists and it came out at 0.
2. Wikipedia and Timewave Zero are the same instrument returning opposite verdicts on
   structurally similar systems. If the cabinet can articulate *what* differs beyond
   "one has sources," that is *A Consistent Fiction* v0.2's missing worked example.
3. The **derived** column has three entries and two of them are physical. If every
   derived specimen in the world is physically anchored, that is either a strong result
   about the operator or a strong limit on it, and the corpus currently claims neither.

## 5. Why this is not publishable yet

Four exhibits are marked ~ or ?. One of those depends on sources I have not checked. The
program's own checklist runs the prior-art and citation check *before* publication, not
after, and *Gluing the Gates* is the standing reminder of what happens otherwise.

The threshold I would set: **eight exhibits, all ✓, at least three of which the
instruments were expected to pass.** Below that the cabinet is a trophy case and reads
as one.

When it clears that bar it is arguably the most legible surface the program has — the
corpus's instruments applied to objects readers already have opinions about, which is a
better entry point than any of the nineteen documents. That is an argument for building
it carefully, not quickly.

## 6. What is declined

- **No claim that the diagnoses are novel.** Proof of stake's circularity and the
  h-index's Goodhart failure are both well-trodden. The cabinet's contribution is that
  one instrument returns all of them, not that any single verdict is new.
- **No ranking.** Exhibits are not scored against each other. τ is not comparable across
  systems that stake different quantities, and pretending otherwise would be exactly the
  scalarisation *Omnium* objects to.
- **No completeness.** Ten specimens chosen because the instruments say something about
  them, which is a selection effect and is not corrected for.
- **The cabinet does not discharge any open problem.** It is calibration, not research,
  and it goes behind ι and Conjecture 3.1 like everything else that isn't on the
  critical path.
