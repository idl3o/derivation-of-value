## Abstract

The question this paper answers was put to the program from outside its own vocabulary: how close to a *perpetual* mechanism a negentropy-attested measurement can be made. The program contains the impossibility already. A measuring mechanism that runs forever at no cost is Maxwell's demon, and *Proof of Preservation* v0.1 rehearses the exorcism — the demon runs a cycle only by clearing its notes, and clearing costs *kT* log 2 per bit, forever. Perpetual measurement is therefore excluded by the same law the anthology stakes, and the honest question is the asymptote. This paper gives the asymptote a definition and a decomposition. The definition: perpetuity, in the only sense the second law permits, is not the absence of recurring cost but the absence of a *half-life* in whatever prices it — a mechanism is asymptotically perpetual when every cost it pays forever is set by a constant of nature or paid by the sky, and every cost set by an institution is paid once. The decomposition: the four anchors of *Gauge-Fixing the Section Space* v0.2, plus the mechanism's memory, sort into four **amortization classes** — *sky-funded* (the beacon: recurring, but the universe pays), *genesis-paid* (the dissipation floor: once per identity, amortizing to zero over an unbounded horizon), *constant-floor* (erasure: deferrable by append-only design, irreducible at the Landauer wage, priced by a constant that is the same for everyone forever), and *wound* (the delay chain and the unique encoding: sequential time spent freshly every epoch, the one expenditure that neither amortizes nor comes from the sky). The classification yields a convergence the paper regards as its finding: the class that must be paid forever is exactly the anchor *Borrowed Hardness* v0.3 marked as the program's most quantum-fragile load-bearing piece, and — in the one place the corpus has measured — exactly where the trace gap of *Sign and Work* v0.3 is bought. The place a mechanism must be wound is the place it can be wounded, and the paper argues this is not a coincidence but a consequence: sequential time is the single input that no reservoir delivers and no constant prices, so it must be attested rather than presented, and attestation is the layer that is only ever engineering.

## 1. The Demand and the Refusal

A perpetual mechanism for measuring is an old machine with a precise name. A device that measures its environment forever, at no ongoing cost, extracting a usable signal from each observation and returning to measure again, is Maxwell's demon — not an analogue of it but the thing itself, since the demon's whole employment is measurement in a loop. The second volume rehearsed the century it took to price that loop. Szilard located the wage in the bit; Landauer moved it to the forgetting; Bennett closed the cycle: measurement can in principle be performed reversibly, but the demon measures *again* only after clearing its notes, and erasure dissipates at least *kT* log 2 per bit, irrecoverably. The cost of measuring forever is not in the looking. It is in the making-room-to-look-again, and it cannot be evaded because logical irreversibility is thermodynamic irreversibility.

So the program refuses the demand in its strong form before this paper begins, and the refusal is load-bearing elsewhere in the corpus — a mechanism that claimed perpetual measurement would be claiming order minted for free, which is the exact claim the negentropy substrate exists to make expensive. What survives the refusal is the asymptotic question. A mechanism cannot measure forever for nothing; the open matter is what the *cheapest sustainable posture* looks like, and whether the program's own construction is already near it.

## 2. Perpetuity Without the Perpetual

The first volume's coda supplies the redefinition, read at a new layer. Every borrowed scarcity, it found, borrows from an institution with a half-life: energy is dear while extraction is governed, capital while issuance is, coherence while capable witnesses are few. The half-life is the tell. A cost whose price is set by an institution will change when the institution does, and an institution always eventually does.

There is exactly one kind of recurring cost with no half-life: a cost priced by a constant of nature. The Landauer wage is *kT* log 2 per bit for every agent, in every century, under every regime, on every hardware — the second volume closed on precisely this sentence, that the price of what does not spend is a physical constant, the same for everyone, forever. A mechanism that pays such a cost every epoch is not perpetual in the vulgar sense. It is perpetual in the only sense the second law permits: nothing about the passage of time, the turnover of institutions, or the arrival of new machines changes what it owes.

The definition this paper works with is therefore: **a measuring mechanism is asymptotically perpetual when every cost it must pay recurrently is either priced by a physical constant or paid by a reservoir it does not maintain, and every cost priced by an institution is paid a bounded number of times.** Perpetuity is an amortization property, not an energy property. The audit that follows is an amortization audit.

## 3. Four Amortization Classes

The four anchors of *Gauge-Fixing the Section Space* v0.2, together with the mechanism's memory — which the anchor paper does not price, and which this accounting must — sort cleanly.

### 3.1 Sky-funded: the beacon

The astrophysical beacon is the only component of the construction that is perpetual in the strong sense, and it achieves this by the only route available: someone else pays. Pulsars keep their time and the stars wheel around Sgr A* whether or not any mechanism watches; the photons arrive unbidden, unpredictable, and free, and each epoch's challenges are seeded from a source the mechanism spends nothing to maintain. The grinding gauge is fixed in perpetuity at zero recurring cost *to the mechanism* — the qualification §5 will collect its price on. It is consistent, and worth marking, that the same anchor is the one *Borrowed Hardness* clears without remainder: Shor has no purchase on a star. The component that costs nothing forever is also the component that nothing dissolves. Both properties have the same source — the anchor was never the mechanism's to maintain, so it was never the mechanism's to lose.

### 3.2 Genesis-paid: the dissipation floor

The dissipation floor prices the minting of an identity, once, at entry. Over any bounded horizon it is a real expenditure; over the unbounded horizon a perpetuity audit contemplates, a bounded cost per persistent identity amortizes to zero. The anchor paper's own §4.4 anticipated the limit case without naming it in these terms: the floor "may be retired by governance once the identity set has thermalized," which is to say the construction already treats this cost as one that stops. In amortization terms the floor is a boundary condition, not a running cost — it belongs to the mechanism's genesis the way an activation energy belongs to a reaction, paid to reach the state in which the ongoing chemistry is cheap.

### 3.3 Constant-floor: memory and the wage of forgetting

The demon's cost was never in measuring; it was in clearing the notes, and a ledger is a demon that has decided not to clear them. An append-only chain defers the Landauer wage — every bit retained is a bit not yet erased, the erasure cost pushed into storage rather than paid as heat. Deferral is not escape. A mechanism with bounded state must forget, and each forgotten bit costs *kT* log 2; a mechanism with unbounded state has converted a thermodynamic cost into a storage obligation that grows without bound, which is the same debt on different terms. The irreducible recurring cost of the measurement cycle is therefore the wage on the bits the mechanism genuinely cannot keep — and this cost, uniquely among the recurring costs, is priced by the constant the whole program stakes. It has no half-life, no issuer, no regime. By the definition of §2 it is the model citizen of a perpetual mechanism's budget: a cost that is paid forever and can never be repriced.

### 3.4 Wound: sequential time

Two anchors remain, and they refuse both amortization and the sky. The delay chain certifies that each epoch's response took real, elapsed, sequential time; the unique encoding certifies that it took *distinct* sequential time per identity. Both are certificates of having-been-there, and having-been-there is indexed to the epoch — this epoch's elapsed time is no evidence about the next. The expenditure cannot be made in advance (that is the grinding attack the beacon forecloses), cannot be inherited from a previous epoch (that is the backdating attack the chain forecloses), and cannot be delivered by any reservoir, because sequential time is not a substance the sky emits but a relation the mechanism must freshly instantiate. It is the one input that must be *spent by the mechanism itself, every epoch, forever*.

This is the winding. A clock's escapement does not amortize; the mainspring is not priced by a constant of nature but by the arm that turns the key; and a clock that is not wound is not a slow clock but a stopped one. The construction of *Gauge-Fixing* is, in the amortization audit, three parts perpetual and one part clockwork — sky-funded challenge, genesis-paid identity, constant-floor memory, and a temporal core that some arm must crank every epoch or the certificates of having-been-there cease to exist.

## 4. The Winding Point Is the Wound

The title reads two ways and both are meant.

*Borrowed Hardness* v0.3 ran the quantum machine down the substrates and found that negentropy is where it draws blood: the delay chain, built from repeated squaring in a group whose order must be hard to know, collapses under Shor in the RSA groups first reached for, and the repairs — class groups, isogenies — are unbroken rather than proven. The temporal anchor is "the single most quantum-fragile load-bearing piece in the program." That audit was a durability audit; it asked what dissolves. This paper's audit is an amortization audit; it asks what recurs. The two audits were run from unrelated directions, and they converge on the same anchor. The component that must be paid for forever is the component most likely to need re-basing mid-flight.

The convergence has a reason, and stating it is the point of the paper. Sort the mechanism's inputs by where they come from. What the sky delivers — challenge entropy — arrives as physics and needs no attestation; the photons are their own credential. What a constant prices — the erasure wage — is enforced by the law itself; no adversary underpays it, because underpaying it is what the second law forbids. But sequential time is neither delivered nor enforced. It is *real* — the second volume counted it first among the shadows order casts — yet it reaches the mechanism only through a certificate, and the certificate is cryptography, and cryptography is the layer *Borrowed Hardness* found to be only ever engineering: durable by re-basing, never by derivation. The recurring cost that cannot be handed to the sky or to a constant must be handed to a primitive, and primitives are exactly where the machine bites. The winding point and the wound coincide because both mark the same boundary — the place where the mechanism stops presenting physics and starts asserting it.

There is one measurement in the corpus that bears on this, and it points the same way. *Sign and Work* v0.3 §5 found that the trace gap — the ratio of forging a reading to earning it — is bought entirely by the anchors and, among the anchors modelled, tracks the *per-section production cost*: the recurring, per-epoch, per-identity expenditure of §3.4's class, with the gap approaching soundness as that cost dominates. Output constraints, pinned frames, the whole apparatus of things paid once — Prop 5.1 shows they open no gap at all. One instrument on one substrate is not a theorem, and the caution of that paper's §6 carries over unreduced. But as far as the corpus has measured, the mechanism's security per epoch is purchased with precisely the coin that must be re-spent every epoch. The clockwork is not an unfortunate residue of the design. It is where the design keeps its soundness.

## 5. What Is Declined

Four flattering readings are declined by name.

**That the sky is perpetual.** It is prepaid, which is different. A pulsar is a flywheel spinning down; the orbits around Sgr A* are a bound system radiating; the beacon is a slow spend of the largest ordered reservoirs in view, and the mechanism free-rides on an expenditure it did not make. The timescales are astronomical against any horizon a mechanism will see, which is why §3.1 stands for every practical purpose. But the honest form of the claim is that the beacon has a half-life too — merely one so long that no institution's compares — and a program that stakes the second law does not get to exempt its favourite anchor from it. Sky-funded means the bill is real and addressed to someone else.

**That the floor's retirement is free.** §3.2 leaned on *Gauge-Fixing* §4.4's provision that governance may retire the dissipation floor once the identity set has thermalized, and governance is an institution, and institutions are the thing this paper's definition of perpetuity exists to exclude. A genesis cost retired by a committee has a half-life smuggled back into the budget at the moment of retirement. The amortization argument survives — a bounded cost amortizes whether or not it is ever formally retired — but the *retirement* is an institutional act and should be priced as one.

**That the Landauer floor is a price the mechanism pays.** It is a bound the mechanism approaches. Real erasure on real hardware dissipates orders of magnitude above *kT* log 2, and nothing in this paper closes that engineering distance. The constant-floor class names the direction of the asymptote and the currency it is denominated in; it does not name today's price. A mechanism citing this paper as evidence that its running costs are physical constants would be doing the flattery the program forbids.

**That the winder is inside the mechanism.** A clock presupposes an arm. Someone computes the delay chain every epoch, and the mechanism must make it worth their while, and a reward denominated in the mechanism's own token is circular at bootstrap in exactly the way *Gauge-Fixing* §4.4 found stake to be — the winding is worth paying for only if the mechanism works, and the mechanism works only if the winding is paid for. The circle is presumably breakable the same way, by exogenous subsidy at genesis that endogenous reward later replaces, but *presumably* is the operative word: the incentive design of the winding is unbuilt, unpriced, and not addressed here. This paper has classified the mechanism's costs. It has not shown that anyone will pay them.

## 6. Coda

The universe spends itself, and a mechanism that would measure forever has, at bottom, three honest strategies: spend what the universe was already spending, spend what a constant prices identically forever, or spend once and be done. The construction the program has assembled uses all three — the beacon, the wage, the floor — and then meets the input for which no strategy exists. Sequential time cannot be free-ridden, cannot be priced by law, cannot be prepaid. It must be spent, freshly, by the mechanism, every epoch, and attested by a primitive that the durability volume has already marked as the thinnest in the program.

So the closest thing to a perpetual measuring mechanism the framework admits is a Szilard engine run at the Landauer limit with the sky as its randomness bath and a clock at its core that some arm must wind. Three of its four costs will never be repriced by any institution, any adversary, or any machine. The fourth is the mechanism's mortality, kept in one place where the program can watch it: the winding point, which is the wound. That the two coincide is not the construction failing. It is the construction telling the truth about where a mechanism ends and physics begins — and a mechanism that knows the location of its own mortality, to the anchor, is nearer to perpetual than one that has been promised it will live forever.
