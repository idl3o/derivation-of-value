Somewhere between two buildings there is a path across the grass that nobody designed. It was not surveyed, not approved, not costed; it exists because people walked, and walking wears. No one was consulted and no one agreed to anything. The path is not a record of a decision to walk — there was no decision — it is the walking itself, still there after the walkers have gone. And it cannot be counterfeited. Someone who wanted such a path and did not want to walk would have to wear the grass some other way, which is to say would have to do the work, which is to say would have walked.

This volume is about that difference: between what an action *claims* and what an action *leaves*, and the possibility that value should be staked on the second rather than the first.

Four volumes have now named substrates — the structural agreement of many models, the order a system holds against its thermal grave, the silent dimensions an instrument of value carries, the magnitude at which a people can act — and every one of them arrived, by its own road and in its own vocabulary, at the same wall. The substrate was real. The substrate was scarce. The substrate was indifferent to which regime happened to be in power. And the substrate did not say so. Something else had to say so on its behalf, and that something was a meter, an inspector, a certificate, a form; and every one of those is a place where the mechanism stops measuring and starts trusting. The wall was never the substrate. It was the attestation.

*The Same Line, Drawn Four Times*
{:.section-title}

The program has drawn one line four times in four vocabularies without noticing it was the same line.

The capacity volume drew it first, though it took a later revision to see. A civilization's outward magnitude — the joules it captures, the operations it performs — is a quantity of flow, and a flow is gone the moment it has passed. Nothing of the megawatt-hour remains for anyone to examine; there is only the record, and a record is a report, and a report needs a reporter. But a civilization that can place atoms *has placed them*, and what it placed is still there, and anyone with an instrument fine enough may go and look. One rung is attested by testimony and the other by the thing itself.

The framework drew it second, working on adversaries rather than on civilizations. A projection can be harvested — a failed attack on it can be made to subsidise the substrate it attacked — exactly when the attempt leaves behind something of the same kind as honest work leaves. Where the attempt produces only a false report, rejection erases it and nothing remains to keep. Where the attempt must move matter, the matter stays whether or not the claim is believed.

The identity paper drew it third. An identity asserted is free, and identities that are free make vacuous every bound that counts witnesses. An identity that must leave its own trace is not free, and the count means something again.

And the fourth drawing has not been written yet but its subject is old: the termites of the entomologists, who build without a plan and without instructions, each depositing where deposits already are, so that the structure is not directed by any of them and is produced by all of them. Coordination through the medium; the trace as the only message.

Capacity, harvest, identity, coordination. Four problems, four papers, four sets of words. One line, drawn between what is asserted and what is left.

*Assertion and Residue*
{:.section-title}

Name the two sides and the reason for the program's difficulty becomes visible.

An **assertion** is a claim *about* an action. It is separable from the action — that is what makes it a claim — and being separable, it can be produced without the action, which is the whole of the forgery problem. Assertions are cheap in exactly the way information is cheap: to copy an assertion costs nothing, and to fabricate one costs no more than to make a true one. Every mechanism resting on assertion must therefore find someone to vouch, and the voucher is the oracle, and the oracle is the point at which the mechanism's security ceases to be its own.

A **residue** is not a claim about an action. It is a part of the world the action changed. It does not describe the walking; it is the worn grass. Nobody vouches for it because there is nothing to vouch — one goes and measures, and one's measurement agrees with everyone else's, not because the parties trust each other but because they are all looking at the same object. This is exactly what the framework demands when it requires a verifier that appeals to nothing outside the protocol's own state, and exactly what the negentropy volume meant by many witnesses checking one another rather than one meter asked to be honest.

The distinction is not that residues are more reliable than assertions. It is that residues do not require reliability *of anyone*. That is a different and much stronger property, and it is the one the program has been reaching for since its first volume, in four different disguises.

*Why the Involuntary Can Be Trusted*
{:.section-title}

There is a reason a trace is credible, and it is worth stating precisely, because it is the security argument and not a sentiment.

A residue is *involuntary*. The walker did not choose to leave the path; the path is what walking does. And this cuts both ways, which is the whole point. One cannot do the work without leaving the residue — that is what makes the residue *complete*, a record with no gaps to hide in. And one cannot leave the residue without doing the work — that is what makes it *sound*, since the only route to the trace runs through the labour.

Both halves are needed and they are different claims. A record that could be produced some other way is a proxy, and proxies get eaten; the anthology has said so from its first volume and has watched it happen. A record that the work might fail to produce is a partial record, and partial records are where a forger lives. Only when the two hold together does the trace become the thing the program has wanted all along: a quantity that is not *reported* to the mechanism but *presented* to it.

Notice what this does to the oldest problem in the program. The failed promise of useful work foundered because verifying that the useful work was genuinely done is the difficulty the whole scheme was trying to escape — the verification problem is the consensus problem in another costume. Residue does not solve that problem. It *dissolves* the shape of it, by staking a thing which is not verified at all in the sense the objection intends. There is nothing to check the claim against, because there is no claim. There is an object, and the object is either there or it is not.

*The Substrate the Second Volume Was Reaching For*
{:.section-title}

Honesty now requires a deflation, and the deflation is more interesting than the claim it deflates.

A residue is a low-entropy structure that persists. That is what a trace *is*: order left where there was less order, holding for a while against the general dissolution. Which means the substrate this volume has been circling is not a fifth thing standing beside coherence, negentropy, dimensional integrity, and capacity. **It is negentropy with its provenance fixed.**

The second volume named order-against-entropy and could not make it attest itself, and the paper that followed spent its length on exactly the gap: fixing the temporal freedom so a section carries a history and not merely a state, fixing the identity freedom so a duplicated section fails to glue, fixing the grinding freedom, pricing the minting of witnesses. Read from here, those anchors were never a security patch bolted onto negentropy. They were the operation that turns negentropy into residue — that takes a quantity of order and binds it to *whose* order it is and *when* it came to be. Order without provenance is a number anyone may claim. Order with provenance is a trace.

So the anthology has named more substrates than it has. What look like five namings are fewer, and the reduction runs along a line the program can now see: the substrates genuinely differ — coherence is a relation among models and capacity is a magnitude of action and these are not the same thing — but **the attestation problem does not differ**. It is one problem wearing the local costume of whatever is being staked. And residue is its general answer, which is precisely why it kept appearing in four papers that thought they were about four different things.

This is a smaller claim than the volume began with and a better one. The program does not gain a substrate here. It gains the knowledge of what it has been solving.

*What Leaves Nothing*
{:.section-title}

A volume that stopped there would be doing the flattery the program forbids, so name the failures.

Most of what matters leaves nothing. A decision not taken leaves nothing. A message withheld leaves nothing; censorship is invisible precisely because its product is an absence, and no amount of measuring finds a thing that was never made. Liveness leaves nothing — a system that is simply *up*, doing what it should, produces no artefact distinguishable from a system that was never asked. The framework's harvest results already carry this restriction and it is worth repeating in the essay's register: residue answers forgery and says nothing whatever about denial. An adversary who cannot fake can still refuse, and refusal is the older attack.

Residues can also be laid down cheaply if the design is careless. The security argument rested on a biconditional — no work without the trace, no trace without the work — and the second half is a *design obligation*, not a gift. A mechanism that scores a residue an adversary can produce more cheaply than the labour it is meant to evidence has built a proxy and will watch it be eaten, exactly as the program has been warning since the first volume. Nothing about residue makes a mechanism careful.

And residues decay. This is the sharpest limit and the most fitting, since it returns the volume to the sentence the anthology has repeated from its beginning. The universe spends itself. A trace is a local and temporary refusal to be spent, and the refusal is not permanent: grass grows back, lattices anneal, the path fades when the walking stops. To stake residue is to stake something with a half-life, and a mechanism resting on it is racing a clock it did not set and cannot stop. The second volume knew this about negentropy. It is no less true of negentropy with its provenance fixed.

---

*The Adversary Leaves It Too*
{:.section-title}

One consequence deserves its own naming, because it is the strangest thing the line implies and the program arrived at it from the other direction.

A residue is involuntary, and involuntary does not distinguish intentions. The adversary who sets out to counterfeit a trace and fails has, in failing, left one — of the same kind as an honest participant's, because that is what the biconditional means. The wearing of the grass does not ask why the walker walked.

So on substrates that admit residue, a failed attack is not a loss to be absorbed. It is a contribution, made by someone who did not intend to make it, and the mechanism keeps it because there is no mechanism by which it could give it back. The framework has this as a proposition with conditions attached; stated plainly, it says the opponent's expended effort accrues to the thing it was expended against, and that this happens by physics rather than by anyone's good will.

The founding move of the anthology was to stop borrowing the staked quantity and derive it. Here is the same move performed on the adversary, who has been treated throughout every mechanism-design tradition as an externality — a thing to be resisted, a cost to be survived. On a substrate of residue the adversary is not outside the system at all. It is a participant that has not consented to participate, and its labour is as real as anyone's, and the trace it leaves is as good.

*Coda*
{:.section-title}

The volumes before this one each named a thing to stake and then spent themselves establishing that the thing was real, scarce, and beyond the reach of any regime. This one has named something smaller and, it turns out, more useful: not a new quantity, but the form an old quantity has to take before any mechanism can read it without asking permission of an oracle.

What is owed is unchanged and should not be understated by the arrival of a vocabulary. Nothing is built. The security claim still waits on a mechanism and an opponent, as the third volume said it would and as it has said twice since. The independence measure on which every quantitative result in the program now depends has resisted two attempts to measure it. And the substrate the anthology most wants — the order of magnitude at which a civilization can act — remains the least verifiable of all, though it now has an axis along which it might one day become verifiable at all, which is more than it had.

The technical companion to this volume will take up the termites: coordination through the medium, the trace as the only message, and the question of whether a mechanism can *cultivate* an interface rather than specify one. It will find, as these papers keep finding, that the substrate was doing the work the designer was being credited with.

The universe spends itself. What does not spend is precious. And what cannot be helped — the mark that the doing makes because the doing makes it, that no one chose and no one may withhold and no one need be trusted to report — is the most honest thing a mechanism will ever be handed, for exactly as long as it lasts.
